Transparency in AI depends on more than just technical capability—it is shaped by the norms, incentives, and regulations that determine how models are built, documented, and shared. As commercialization accelerates, opacity around key upstream inputs, especially training data, is becoming more entrenched. To reverse this trend, researchers, policymakers, and developers must actively embed transparency into governance frameworks, research agendas, and industry standards.

**For researchers**

- Conduct regular audits of model documentation to track changes over time, especially as new governance frameworks emerge.

- Develop case studies highlighting best practices to guide model documentation efforts.

- Explore how transparency levels relate to outcomes such as bias, robustness, and societal impact.

**For policymakers**

- Require minimum disclosure of key metadata fields, including dataset provenance, compute resources, and model reuse, as part of regulatory compliance.

- Promote the adoption of FAIR data principles and machine-readable documentation standards across jurisdictions and sectors (Wilkinson et. al, 2016).

- Embed transparency requirements across the entire model lifecycle, from dataset curation to post-deployment audits.

**For developers and model publishers**

- Use standard templates to disclose training metadata, including compute, hardware, and duration, to enable meaningful comparisons.

- Clearly document model lineage, stating whether the model is original or fine-tuned and referencing base models in structured formats such as model cards or datasheets.

- Indicate the provenance of each metadata field, specifying whether it is self-reported, inferred, or externally verified, and include confidence indicators where possible.

**From insight to impact**

Visualization tools like this dashboard are not just research outputs, they are mechanisms for public accountability. To translate insight into impact, such tools must be integrated into policy cycles, procurement requirements, and industry benchmarks. In the best case, transparency would be measurable, enforceable, and universally expected, with every model released alongside complete, standardized, and verifiable documentation that enables meaningful scrutiny and drives continuous improvement in responsible AI.
