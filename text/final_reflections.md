This dashboard should be seen as a prototype exploration, not a definitive or exhaustive evaluation. While the dataset proved valuable for surfacing broad patterns and comparing transparency across organization types, regions, and access models -- it is not without gaps. Many fields rely on inferred or incomplete metadata, and certain aspects of transparency, such as downstream impacts or finer-grained dataset provenance, remain outside its scope. As such, the insights here should be read in context and treated as indicative rather than conclusive.

The analysis demonstrates that we can meaningfully identify who is more likely to disclose, where transparency lags, and how it varies by model access. Yet moving toward a healthier AI ecosystem will require deeper, more systematic investigations of transparency practices, across a wider range of models, with richer and verified transparent disclosures.

In the best case, transparency would be a baseline expectation: every model released with complete, standardized documentation covering data, compute, environmental impact, and intended use. Such information would be openly verifiable, easily comparable across models, and embedded in shared reporting frameworks adopted by developers, funders, regulators, and publishers alike. This visual analysis offers one step toward that vision, making transparency visible, measurable, and open to public scrutiny, with the ultimate goal of fostering an AI landscape where openness drives trust and continuous improvement.
